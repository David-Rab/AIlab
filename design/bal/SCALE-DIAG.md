## 1. Formula map (player's lab)
**Base G** (`models/effective.ts:86 gPreOf`, `models/capability.ts:161 gOfBuild`):
G = log10(F) + e_pre(F) + shape + log10 f_over(tpp) + log10 f_data + log10 f_qual + dg_design + dG_RL(F).
- (a) **Hardware.** F = 6 N D (`effective.ts:20`); N, D enter G only through F, tpp = D/N (overtraining `fOver` :28, 400 tpp = -0.34, 2000 = -0.80, 20000 = -1.80) and repeated data (`fData` :66, Besiroglu law). **No size ceiling** other than those: slope dG/dlog F = 1 exactly at fixed stacks.
- RL: dG_RL = mean over domains of A/(1+(C_mid/C_RL)^B), A = 2.0 OOM, B = 0.33, C_mid = c0 (F/3e26)^0.5 / 10^e_RL, C_RL = 0.15 F (`effective.ts:151-230`, `runDesign.ts:RL_CURVE`). Adds 0.12-0.19 OOM per OOM of F (G slope 1.12-1.19 with no research). Bounded by the ceiling.
- E_START = 1.19 (`runDesign.ts:20`), a recipe at start.
- (b) **Stacks.** e_pre(F) = base + sum over adopted ideas a_i 10^(s_i log10 F), a_i = x_i 10^(-s_i log10 F_test) (`stacks.ts:44-51, 100-111`). Stacks rlMid, rlCeil move C_mid and A; train (cap 0.26) and serve are throughput.
- (c) **Scale lever** = the F^s factor: an idea measured at test scale F_test = testFrac x latest base (lines 0.002, ideas 0.01; `data/v2/research.ts:LINE_STAGES`) adds x_i (F/F_test)^s_i OOM. s per idea ~ N(mu, sd) per node (`tree.ts:142-145`: L.scale 0.05/0.04, L.data 0/0.05, L.opt -0.25/0.20, L.latent 0.05/0.04), clamped to S_RANGE = [-0.6, +0.25] (`research.ts:100`). A scale-test survives if x_test >= 0.5 x_small, x_test = x_small 1000^s (`SMALL_TO_TEST` 1000, `SURVIVE_FRAC` 0.5, `models/research.ts:atTest`, `flowSim`), so survivors are selected on high s. One-off ideas (P24 etc.) have no `s` (scale-invariant). Public inflow `addPublic`: scale-invariant.
- So **dG/dlog F = 1 + dG_RL/dlog F + ln10 sum_i s_i a_i F^s_i**. The last term is proportional to the stock of adopted ideas, and the exponent acts on the OOM effect itself.
- (d) **Students.** G_student = G_teacher - delta (`effective.ts:269 distillGap`, `runs.ts:332 distillBuild`), delta = 0.19 + 0.92 log10(N_T/N_S) + 0.2 x OOM of tokens below 20/param. Table: 1/2 0.47, 1/6 0.91 (cheap tier, `cheapTier.ts`), 1/8 1.02, 1/80 1.94, 1/1000 2.95. Independent of teacher G, no size ceiling. Then **rule B** adds `distillLift` = 0.5 x access x fit(N_S) x (G_best_released_other_lab - own G) to every post-training (`runs.ts:933`, `diffusion.ts:97`, `calibration.ts:13-16`); fit 0.35 / 0.7 / 0.9 at 1.5B / 7B / 32B. A from-scratch small base gets log10 F lower by 2 log10(N ratio) (F ~ N^2 at fixed tpp), the stacks in full (e_pre at its small F) and rule B.

## 3. Sources (primary)
- **Scale-dependence.** Gundlach et al., "On the Origin of Algorithmic Progress in AI", arXiv 2511.21622 (v1 Nov 2025): ablations "account for less than 10x of these gains at small compute scales" of Ho et al.'s 22,000x; "an algorithm's efficiency gains are tied to compute scale"; LSTM to Transformer CEG 7.9x (loss 3.4), 29x (loss 2.5), 77x at 2017 compute, 352x extrapolated to 2023 compute (1.3e22 FLOP); Kaplan to Chinchilla 3.7x at 2023 compute; the two scale-dependent changes are 88% of extrapolated log gains; scale-invariant ones together 7.9x (21.7x if multiplied: exponent 0.67); "gains estimated at large compute scales can substantially overstate those available at smaller scales"; smaller labs see progress "much more similar to hardware progress"; 2.1x/yr total vs Ho 2.83x/yr (research/20-model-v3-calibration.md:217-249, :759). Caveat: ablations at small scale, fitted and extrapolated.
- **Form of the dependence:** the *multiplier* (CEG) grows with compute: 7.9x at about 1e15 to 352x at 1.3e22 is 0.9 to 2.55 OOM over 7.1 OOM of compute = **+0.23 OOM per OOM of compute, additive in log C** (research/20:254). In the game's form x (F/F_test)^s on the OOM effect the same two points give s = log10(2.83)/7.1 = **0.064**.
- **Capability vs compute.** Epoch Rosetta/ECI: Cap = 0.39 log10(C_eff) + b, k 0.36-0.51 per OOM, so capability is about linear in log compute (research/20:A2 table, :35); loss law L = 1.82 + 482/N^0.348 + 2085/D^0.366 (Besiroglu et al. 2024 refit; reducible loss at the 2025 frontier is about 0.06); data repetition: Muennighoff et al. 2023 (<=4 epochs about free).
- **Algorithmic rate.** Ho et al. 2024: compute for fixed performance halves every 8 months (5-14), about 3x/yr (research/20:210). AIFM: effective compute = training compute + software efficiency, additive in OOM and scale-invariant (`aifm-runs/repo/progress_model/_impl.py:1077-1081`: `etsp_rates = sw_progress_rates + training_system_performance_growth_rate`, `etsp = tc_abs + se`).
- **Small and distilled models.** Epoch (Somala, Emberson, 15 Aug 2025), https://epoch.ai/data-insights/consumer-gpu-model-gap: models that fit one consumer GPU (<=28B on a 4090, <=40B on a 5090) match the frontier "after an average lag between 6-12 months" (GPQA-D 7.4, MMLU-Pro 7.3, AA index 6.3, LM Arena 12.4 months; likely understated for real tasks). Epoch, 29 May 2026, https://epoch.ai/data-insights/open-closed-eci-gap: best open-weight models lag closed ones "by an average of four months", 8 ECI points. Xiao et al., Densing Law of LLMs, arXiv 2412.04315: maximum capability density of open base models doubles about every 3.3 months (3.5 months, Feb 2023 to Apr 2025, Nature Machine Intelligence). DeepSeek-R1 distills (arXiv 2501.12948): 32B / 7B / 1.5B reach 91% / 70% / 36% of R1 on AIME, and "advancing beyond the boundaries of intelligence may still require more powerful base models" (research/20:380-395). Busbridge et al., Distillation Scaling Laws, arXiv 2502.08606: a capacity gap (a stronger teacher can give a worse student) and supervised training wins given enough student compute; studied to 12.6B parameters (research/20:397-405).

## 4. Comparison
1. **Hardware double count: yes, in effect, though not the same FLOP twice.** G already has slope 1 per OOM of F (the AIFM has 1 too). The lever adds a second, research-proportional return to F: slope 1 + 0.2 (2026.5) to 1.4-3.5 (2027.5). In the AIFM a bigger run adds exactly its OOM; here the base growing x6 in year 2 adds +0.3 OOM (central) to +0.9 (fast) of "software" that the research did not make (SHAPE-DIAG §1). It is a hardware x software cross term with no AIFM counterpart, and it rises with the stock of ideas (accumulating "software OOM" make later hardware worth more). The rebalancing half of Gundlach's scale-dependence (Kaplan to Chinchilla, 3.7x) is already modelled explicitly (tpp, `fOver`, `OPT_TPP`) and is in E_START; L.scale's description ("architecture and compute rebalancing at scale") counts it again.
2. **Is scale-dependence sourced? Direction yes, size and form no.** Gundlach: 88% of the 2017-23 log gain was two one-off paradigm changes; the post-2017 routine ideas are scale-invariant and small (7.9x together; most <2x). The game gives every L.scale idea s = 0.05 +- 0.04, i.e. each is a Transformer-grade scale-dependent idea (Transformer-equivalent s = 0.064), when the paper says these are rare ("a paradigm tier, one every 2-4 years", research/20:254 [G]). The tail is worse: S_RANGE max 0.25 is justified in `research.ts:163` by "the Transformer's ≈ 0.23, Gundlach", but 0.23 is the additive slope of the multiplier; applied as an exponent on the OOM effect it is 4x the paradigm's own s = 0.064. The high-s survivors (buckets s >= 0.16, probably L.opt: sd 0.2) carry 0.8 of the 1.26 lever OOM at 2027.5; an exponent on the OOM stock makes returns convex in F without limit (F^0.25: x1.8 per OOM).
3. **Early returns too strong? Slightly (in the lever), not in the base.** At 2025.5 the slope is 1.12-1.19 with no lever: matches linear-in-log capability (Epoch 0.39 ECI/OOM) and the AIFM; "no visible limit" at 1e24-1e27 is sourced (there the loss is within 0.06 of its floor and capability still moves with log C; the game's brakes are data repetition and the RL ceiling, not loss). By 2026.5 the lever lifts the slope to 1.3-1.5, and with a few fortunate high-s ideas the player has convex returns the sources do not support (Gundlach's frontier gains are an additive 0.23/OOM for one paradigm).
4. **Late small models too capable? Yes, through three unsourced features.** (i) delta depends only on the size ratio, not on how far the teacher is ahead, and has no capacity ceiling (loss law floor above; Busbridge capacity gap; R1: "may still require more powerful base models"); (ii) the time lag = delta / G-pace, so it shrinks with pace (1.3 months at 4.7 G/yr) against Epoch's 6-12 months for consumer-size models (delta at 1/10 size is 1.1 OOM = 11 months at the start pace 1.2/yr: **correct at the start**); (iii) the stacks apply in full to small runs and rule B adds 0.5 x fit x gap on top. Gundlach: gains at small scale are "much more similar to hardware progress", so a 1e24 run should not keep 64% of the frontier's algorithmic gain once most of it is scale-dependent; but the AIFM (scale-invariant) says it keeps all. Densing law (capability density x2 per 3.3 months) is compatible with (iii)'s speed (about 1 OOM of parameters a year).

## 5. Proposals
**Mechanism (designer decides):**
- M1. **Lever form.** Replace the exponent on the OOM stock by the sourced additive form: idea effect(F) = x + delta log10(F/F_test) (equivalently x (1 + k log10(F/F_test)), k = s ln10, linear in log F, so convexity in F disappears and the lever no longer grows with the stock x). Keep scale-dependence only for a rare paradigm tier (Gundlach 2 of ~20 identified innovations; P24 / latent line), routine ideas scale-invariant.
- M2. **Drop the rebalancing duplicate:** L.scale's "compute rebalancing" is tpp/`fOver`; rename or take the scale term out of that line.
- M3. **Student ceiling:** G_student <= teacher - delta and <= log10 C_floor(N) + recipe, with C_floor from the loss law (`log10ComputeForLoss(E + A/N^alpha)`, `effective.ts:43`; recipe enters as N_eff = N 10^(recipe/2) at fixed tpp: needs a design call), so a small model's capability saturates with size. Rule B lift likewise capped by the same ceiling.
- M4. **Small-run recipe:** the stacks' scale-invariant share applies to small runs; scale-dependent ideas' share shrinks with F (already so for s > 0). No further change unless M1 is rejected.
**Settings (source-derived):**
- S1. S_RANGE max 0.25 -> **0.065** (Gundlach LSTM to Transformer in the game's power form: 0.9 OOM at 1e15 to 2.55 OOM at 1.3e22 FLOP). Lever at the 2027.5 latest base 1.22 -> 0.48 (static what-if; needs a re-run with the re-drawn ideas).
- S2. L.scale mean 0.05 -> about 0.03 (below the paradigm's 0.064: [D], since only ~10% of ideas are paradigm-grade); L.opt sd 0.2 -> 0.06 (so survivors cannot reach 0.25); lever then 0.20 at 2027.5 and +0.07 in 2026.5. If M1 is not adopted this is the minimum fix.
- S3. distill a = 0.19, b = 0.92 stays (fits R1 and Epoch's 6-12 months at 1.2 G/yr); a pace-dependent lag is the model's own consequence, to be checked against the Epoch 4-month open gap after the lever fix, not forced.
- S4. Re-run SHAPE-DIAG's year-2 accounting after S1/S2: its +0.30 (central) to +0.89 (fast) lever term is the quantity these proposals remove or cut by 60-80%.
